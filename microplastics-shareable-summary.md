# Shareable Summary: Microplastics in Drinking Water (What Actually Works)

*A calm, 1-minute cheat sheet based on peer-reviewed lab studies (2023–2025) — easy to paste into a family chat, neighborhood group, or forum.*

---

**First, don't panic:** Health agencies like the WHO and U.S. EPA note we still don't have proven human health thresholds for microplastics in drinking water. You don't need an expensive system—just avoid a few common traps.

### What Lab Studies Actually Show

1. **Standard water pitchers (like basic loose-carbon filters) aren't built for microplastics.**
   University of Toronto testing (Cherian et al., 2023) found pitchers with only loose carbon + resin were inconsistent and sometimes released *more* particles than went in. Look for a **physical membrane** (~0.2 µm, like a dual-stage membrane pitcher) or an **NSF/ANSI 401 certified** solid carbon block (~0.5–1 µm).
2. **Boiling tap water has a big catch.**
   A 2024 study (Yu et al., *ES&T Letters*) found boiling **hard** water traps 80–90% of plastics inside limescale—*if* you strain out the white scale (soft water only removed ~25%). **Two warnings:**
   - Boiling in a **plastic electric kettle** sheds millions to billions of *new* nanoplastics (Shi et al., 2025). Use 100% stainless steel or glass (watch for plastic windows/lids!).
   - Never boil water to fix **lead, nitrates, or PFAS**—boiling concentrates them (EPA/CDC).
3. **Filtered tap water beats bottled water on both cost and plastics.**
   A 2024 Columbia study (*PNAS*) estimated ~240,000 particles/L in bottled water (~90% nanoplastics; exact counts remain debated by chemists). Even glass bottles with **painted metal caps** shed polyester paint flakes during factory sealing (ANSES, 2025).
4. **Best practical options by budget:**
   - **Tightest budget + hard water (no lead/PFAS):** Boil in a 100% stainless/glass pot + strain through a paper coffee filter (~$15–$35/yr).
   - **Renters / soft water:** Membrane-equipped pitcher like LifeStraw Home ($40–$60 upfront, ~$50–$90/yr filters).
   - **Homeowners (keeps minerals, zero waste):** Under-sink NSF/ANSI 401 solid carbon block ($130–$350 DIY, ~$70–$150/yr).
   - **Maximum removal (incl. most nanoplastics):** Reverse Osmosis ($200–$550+ DIY, ~$100–$200/yr).

**Tip before buying:** Look up the exact model in the official **NSF, WQA, or IAPMO** directory to confirm **NSF/ANSI 401 for microplastics** (and remember: no consumer standard yet certifies *nanoplastics* <0.1 µm).
