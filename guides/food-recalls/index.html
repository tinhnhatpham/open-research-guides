3. Annual Trend: Total FDA Food Recall Records vs. Class I (2015–2025)

Data literacy note: In openFDA, each individual product/SKU or lot affected by a single contamination outbreak counts as a separate enforcement record. A single large Listeria or Salmonella supplier recall can generate hundreds of records in one year—so spikes often reflect one huge shared supplier rather than a broad collapse in food safety. Also note: openFDA covers FDA-regulated foods (produce, dairy, packaged snacks, seafood), while USDA FSIS separately regulates most meat, poultry, and processed eggs.

4. Calm Household Food-Safety Takeaways

If Nobody in Your Home Has Food Allergies

  • Nearly a quarter of FDA food recalls are undeclared allergen labeling mistakes—they are safe for non-allergic households.
  • Focus your attention on Class I pathogen recalls (Listeria, Salmonella, E. coli), especially for ready-to-eat chilled foods (soft cheeses, deli salads, bagged greens, pre-cut fruit, ice cream).
  • Listeria uniquely grows at refrigerator temperatures—keep your fridge at or below 40°F (4°C) and wipe spills promptly.

If Someone in Your Home Has a Severe Allergy

  • Milk, soy, wheat, egg, and sesame account for the vast majority of undeclared-allergen recalls—most often due to packaging mix-ups or shared bakery/snack lines.
  • Check official alerts at fda.gov/safety/recalls (free email subscription) rather than relying on social media rumors.
  • If a product is recalled, check the exact lot code & best-by date before throwing everything out—most recalls cover narrow production runs.
