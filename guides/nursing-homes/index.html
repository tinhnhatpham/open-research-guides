Tool #3 • FY2024 CMS Form 2540-10 Financial Analysis

3. Follow the Money: Medicaid-Controlled Margins & Direct Labor Spending

Industry lobbyists frequently argue that lower staffing or tighter finances reflect Medicaid payer mix rather than ownership choices. Linking all 13,819 deduplicated FY2024 SNF Cost Reports (Form 2540-10, full-year ≥300-day preference, blank Title XIX days treated as 0 so zero-Medicaid SNFs remain in <40%) proves the gap persists inside every single Medicaid tier:

Medicaid Share Tier Ownership Matched Homes True Patient Op. Margin (Med.) Total Net Margin (Med.) Total Direct Labor / Day W-2 Salaries / Day Net Pat. Rev / Day PBJ Hrs / Day
How to read these two margins & labor metrics: True Patient-Care Operating Margin = Net Income from service to patients / Net Patient Revenue (pure clinical operations). Total Net Margin = Net Income / (Net Patient Revenue + Total Other Income) (includes philanthropy, endowment/investment income, and government transfers that allow non-profits to sustain negative patient-care operating margins while spending +$25 to +$80/day more on direct care). Total Direct Labor / Day = (Adjusted Salaries + Core Wage-Related Costs + Contract Labor) / Total Patient Days, capturing agency/contract nurses alongside W-2 payroll. All operating expense ratios use Less Total Operating Expense (never Total Costs).
Two Critical Accounting Caveats: (1) Related-Party Masking (Worksheet A-8-1): For-profit chains routinely pay rent, management fees, and therapy contracts to sister LLCs or affiliated REITs owned by the same investors. Because public Form 2540-10 summary extracts only partially adjust related-party markups, reported for-profit margins (+1.6% to +3.1% total net) are best viewed as a conservative lower bound on total enterprise cash extraction. (2) Hospital-Based SNF Exclusion: 68.9% (419 of 608) of hospital-based SNFs do not appear in Form 2540-10 because they file under Hospital Cost Report Form 2552-10 instead (vs. 96.8% match rate for freestanding SNFs).

6. Six Concrete Checks for Families Touring a Nursing Home

  1. Visit unannounced on a Saturday or Sunday afternoon: Nationally, Registered Nurse (RN) hours drop 30.2% from the 7-day average to weekends (an implied 37.7% drop from Mon–Fri weekdays to weekends). Sunday afternoon shows the facility's true care floor.
  2. Look beyond the headline 5-star badge: Nearly half (46.8%) of 5-star homes carry at least one major warning sign (weekend drop ≥15%, turnover ≥50%, active fines, or overdue inspection), and 20 homes hold a 4-star rating despite an active Abuse Icon. Use the Lookup above first.
  3. Check the last health inspection date: In Alabama (85.3% overdue), Virginia (57.1%), and DC (56.2%), a 5-star rating may rest on a pre-pandemic 2020 inspection—four active 5-star AL homes have not had a standard health survey since 2020.
  4. Verify who actually operates a "Government" facility in TX or IN: 286 county/hospital-district nursing homes in Texas (195) and Indiana (91) are leased to private corporate chains and average 81 fewer minutes of care per day than genuine public/veterans' homes.
  5. Read CMS staffing footnotes (especially 23, 24, 25): Footnote 24 (274 homes) means high days with zero RN onsite; 25 (160 homes) means CMS could not validate PBJ staffing accuracy; 23 (122 homes) means the facility failed to submit PBJ staffing data at all.
  6. Read full Form 2567 inspection narratives on ProPublica & Care Compare: Every facility dossier in our Lookup links directly to both Medicare Care Compare and ProPublica Nursing Home Inspect so you can read the verbatim deficiency text before signing admission papers.

7. Prior Art, Methodology & Open Reproducible Data

Credit to Existing Public-Interest Tools & What We Uniquely Add: Medicare Care Compare is the official federal consumer portal; ProPublica Nursing Home Inspect is the gold standard for full-text Form 2567 inspection narratives and deficiency search; and The Oversight Report pioneered independent SNF ownership & cost-report transparency. What this open-source platform uniquely adds is a single, zero-paywall, fully reproducible 5-endpoint CMS pipeline uniting all 14,686 facilities, all 612 chains, a live audit of 14,257 nameless PECOS organization rows, Medicaid-controlled FY2024 true operating margins & total direct labor, the TX/IN government-chain lease distortion, and instant client-side + bulk CSV/JSON downloads.
  1. CMS Provider Information (4pq5-n9py): 14,686 Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing homes (Payroll-Based Journal staffing, turnover, stars, fines, abuse icons, SFF status, footnotes; numeric CCNs zero-padded to 6 chars, 245 alphanumeric CCNs preserved).
  2. CMS Nursing Home Chain Performance Measures: Dynamically resolved via data.cms.gov/data.json (description=="latest"); 612 corporate chains after filtering out the blank-ID National benchmark row.
  3. CMS SNF Enrollments & SNF All Owners (PECOS): 14,401 enrollments → 14,209 matched CCNs joined to 302,718 owner/manager/ADP rows, auditing PE, REIT, ADP, Holding, Management, Investment, Financial, Consulting, Medical Staffing flags and nameless Organization rows (TYPE='O' with blank org name).
  4. CMS SNF Cost Report FY2024 (Form 2540-10): 14,587 raw filings deduplicated to 13,819 matched CCNs (preferring full-year ≥300-day / longest period; blank Title XIX days = 0; using Less Total Operating Expense, never Total Costs; disclosing 68.9% hospital-based Form 2552-10 exclusion).
  5. Staffing Drop Methodology: CMS reports 7-day average HPRD and weekend (Sat–Sun) HPRD. All drops use consistent ratio-of-means: (mean_7day - mean_wknd) / mean_7day. Implied Mon–Fri weekday HPRD is algebraically derived as (7 * mean_7day - 2 * mean_wknd) / 5.
