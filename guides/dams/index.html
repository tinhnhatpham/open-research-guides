Critical Distinction — Hazard vs. Condition: "High-Hazard Potential" does not mean a dam is about to break—it means people live downstream and failure would probably cause loss of life. The urgent public-safety red flags are High-Hazard dams simultaneously rated Poor or Unsatisfactory (2,447 dams) or missing an Emergency Action Plan (3,090 dams)—including 629 dams with both failures at once.

2. Who Owns America's High-Hazard Dams? (The Private-Owner Preparedness Gap)

Nearly half of all U.S. High-Hazard dams (6,269) are privately owned (HOAs, estates, utilities, clubs, ranches)—and 34.8% of private High-Hazard dams lack an Emergency Action Plan, compared to just 4.4% of Federal dams and 4.0% of Public Utility dams.

4. Honest Methodological Caveats & Open Dataset Downloads

Important Notes (Read Before Citing):
  • State Rating Differences: States enforce different dam inspection standards and reporting cadences. A high count of "Poor" dams (e.g., NC, TX, GA, SC, PA) partly reflects active state inspection programs that actually classify deficiencies, whereas some states report many dams as "Not Rated" or "Not Available."
  • Publicly Releasable Scope: USACE's live public nation.csv release covers 62,569 publicly releasable dams (including 14,266 High-Hazard dams); certain security-restricted federal/military structures are omitted by USACE from public bulk files.
