1. Zone X vs. High-Risk Flood Zones (5,000 Paid Claims)

In our live sample of 5,000 paid NFIP claims, 28.7% occurred in Moderate/Low-Risk zones (Zone X/B/C)—matching FEMA's long-run 25%–40% historical range. Equally striking: for insured building claims with documented damage, the median NFIP payout covered 91.7% of assessed building damage. The catastrophic financial gap happens when homes are uninsured, exceed NFIP's $250,000 residential building cap, or lack separate contents coverage.

3. Top States in the Recent OpenFEMA Sample (Showing Zone X Share)

Notice how heavily inland/cloudburst states experience Zone X flooding: in this 5,000-claim sample, 51.6% of Texas claims, 46.2% of Florida claims, and 41.3% of Louisiana claims happened outside mapped high-risk flood zones!

Data literacy note: Because OpenFEMA returns the 5,000 most recent paid claims sorted by date of loss, state rankings reflect recent storm geography rather than 50-year cumulative totals. What remains rock-solid across both this recent snapshot and multi-decade FEMA archives is the high Zone X share (~25%–40%), heavy payout skew, and low contents-payout rate.

4. Primary Sources & Open Reproducibility

  1. FEMA OpenFEMA API v3: https://www.fema.gov/api/open/v3/NfipClaims (5,000 most recent paid residential/commercial NFIP building claims).
  2. FEMA NFIP Policyholder Resources: NFIP Claims Handbook, Severe Repetitive Loss (SRL) Guide, and FloodSmart.gov (residential $250,000 building / $100,000 contents statutory NFIP caps; 60-day Proof of Loss deadline).
  3. Reproducible Code: Full Python script (data/flood-claims/analyze_openfema_flood_claims.py) and output (summary.json) are open in the repository.
