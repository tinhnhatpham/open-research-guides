3. Practical Traveler Protection Checklist

Why wheelchair damage is uniquely severe: Unlike a lost suitcase, a damaged custom power wheelchair can cost $15,000–$40,000+ and leave a traveler stranded or injured for weeks. Choosing a carrier with a sub-0.5% vs. 1.4%+ mishandling rate cuts statistical risk by over two-thirds.

Before You Fly With a Wheelchair / Scooter

  • Book nonstop whenever possible: Transferring mobility devices between connecting planes multiplies handling risk.
  • Photograph every angle at the gate (joysticks, footrests, batteries) right before handing it to ramp staff.
  • Attach laminated handling instructions showing brake release, battery disconnect, and lift points directly onto the frame.
  • Inspect before leaving the airport and file a written mishandling report immediately so the airline must log it under DOT rules.

If Your Flight Is Cancelled or Severely Delayed

  • Check mandatory refund rules: Under DOT rules, if an airline cancels or significantly changes your flight and you choose not to travel, you are entitled to a cash/original-payment refund—not just an expiring voucher.
  • Check FlightRights.gov before booking to see which airlines commit in writing to meals and hotels for controllable delays.
  • File a formal DOT complaint if an airline violates accessibility or refund rules—those filings directly feed the public ATCR dataset and enforcement fines.

4. Primary Sources & Data Caveats

  1. U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) / BTS: Air Travel Consumer Reports (ATCR), Full-Year 2024 and Full-Year 2025 editions (wheelchair/scooter mishandling, domestic on-time performance, cancellation rates, and mishandled baggage).
  2. DOT Aviation Consumer Protection Dashboard: FlightRights.gov (controllable delay/cancellation carrier commitments).
  3. Data caveats: On-time rates vary sharply by geography—airlines concentrated in congested Northeast corridors (like JetBlue) face heavier airspace delays than carriers concentrated in less congested regions. Conversely, wheelchair mishandling reflects ground-handling training and equipment procedures directly under carrier control.
