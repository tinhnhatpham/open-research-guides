4. Four Verified Findings That Cut Through Auto-Safety Myths

1. Hyundai & Kia Dominate U.S. Vehicle Fire Reports (9 of Top 10 Models)

Across 2020–2026 deduplicated complaints, Kia (44.2 fires/1k complaints; 1,100 fires) and Hyundai (30.6/1k; 876 fires) post 5×–7× higher fire intensity than Honda (6.3/1k) or Tesla (5.8/1k), alongside 35 combined Park-Outside fire advisories (21 Hyundai, 14 Kia).

2. Tesla Debunks Both Sides of Internet Hype

Tesla has one of the lowest fire complaint rates of any major brand (5.8 fires/1k complaints; 76 fires)—yet posts 106.4 crash complaints per 1,000 (1,383 crashes out of 12,997 complaints), roughly 3×–4× the crash share of Ford (26.0/1k), Chevrolet (34.2/1k), or Honda (36.2/1k).

3. Ford Leads America in Total Recall Campaigns by More Than 2×

From 2016–2026, Ford logged 657 vehicle recall campaigns (including 77 forced by federal ODI investigations and 18 Do-Not-Drive warnings)—more than double Chevrolet (270) and over triple Toyota (203) and Honda (180).

4. Why Deduplicating NHTSA's Raw CSV Matters

NHTSA repeats the same complaint ID (ODINO) whenever an owner lists multiple failing parts, and repeats campaign unit counts (POTAFF) on every model-year row. Removing 199,155 duplicate ODINO rows cuts naive raw counts from 11,578 fires & 1,666 deaths down to the true 7,121 unique fires & 895 unique fatalities.

Honest Caveats & Open Data Downloads:
  • Unverified Owner Reports & Sales Volume: NHTSA complaints are self-reported by vehicle owners (unverified by crash reconstruction) and naturally scale with how many vehicles a brand sold. Additionally, multi-brand recalls (e.g., Takata airbags) count under each affected brand in the brand table, while national totals deduplicate strictly by unique CAMPNO.
  • Always Check the Specific VIN: A model-year recall or Park-Outside warning may apply only to certain trim engines or factory build dates—always enter the 17-digit VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before buying or driving.
