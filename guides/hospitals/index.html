2. Interactive Patient Tool: Does My Hospital Owe Me Charity Care? (IRS Section 501(r))

Under federal law (IRS Section 501(r)), every tax-exempt non-profit hospital MUST maintain a written Financial Assistance Policy (FAP), publicize it clearly, limit charges for eligible patients to Amounts Generally Billed (AGB), and pause extraordinary collection actions (wage garnishment, lawsuits, credit reporting) while an application is reviewed.

3. Ownership Comparison: Non-Profit vs. For-Profit vs. Government (Acute & CAH S-10)

Across 4,040 Acute Care and Critical Access hospitals filing full-year FY2024 Worksheet S-10 charity care data, tax-exempt non-profits actually post a lower median charity care share (`1.53%`) than for-profits (`2.16%`)—while for-profits charge dramatically higher list markups (`6.40×` vs. `3.84×`) and earn `+6.4%` patient-care operating margins.

4. Five Verified Flagship Findings & Accounting Caveats

Finding 1: The Nonprofit Charity-Care Paradox

31.7% (818 of 2,579) tax-exempt acute/CAH hospitals spend under 1% of operating expenses on charity care, and 60.3% (1,554) spend under 2%. 408 tax-exempt hospitals spend under 1% while posting ≥5% total net margins—including major flagship medical centers (e.g., Stanford Health Care at 0.25% charity care vs. 13.3% / $1.21B net income).

Fairness nuance: Hospitals in Medicaid-expansion states (CA, NY, VT) treat fewer uninsured patients than non-expansion states (TX, FL), which naturally lowers pure charity-care share—though Medicaid shortfalls are separate from free patient bill forgiveness.

Finding 2: The Chargemaster Markup Explosion

Across 4,299 acute/CAH hospitals, the median bill is 3.74× actual cost (1 / Cost-to-Charge Ratio). 30.8% (1,324) charge ≥5× cost and 266 charge 10× to 22.7× cost. For-profits lead at 6.40× median markup vs. 3.84× non-profits and 2.12× government hospitals. Uninsured patients who don't invoke 501(r) or self-pay caps are the only ones billed these raw list prices.

Finding 3: Patient-Care Operating Margin vs. Total Net Margin

Non-profit hospitals show a -1.0% median patient-care operating margin (Net Income from Service to Patients / Net Patient Revenue) yet a +6.1% median Total Net Margin once investment gains, endowments, and other income are included. For-profits earn +6.4% operating / +9.6% net; government hospitals sit at -8.7% operating / +3.8% net (relying on local tax appropriations).

Finding 4: CMS's Hospital Private Equity Disclosure Blind Spot

Unlike nursing homes (which received stricter CMS PECOS PE/REIT reporting rules), CMS's November 2026 Hospital All Owners file (147,029 rows covering 5,154 hospitals) shows only 5 hospitals (0.1%) self-reporting Private Equity ownership and 1 reporting a REIT—dramatically below independent academic/watchdog counts (~400+ PE-owned hospitals).

Finding 5 (Data-Literacy Catch): The Worksheet S-10 Bad-Debt Accounting Trap

In CMS's flat FY2024 Hospital Cost Report extract, Cost of Charity Care is cost-adjusted (multiplied by the hospital's Cost-to-Charge Ratio), whereas Total Bad Debt Expense is reported at inflated billed chargemaster charges ($33.3B raw charges at non-profits!). Comparing raw bad debt directly against charity care cost creates a massive apples-to-oranges distortion. Our pipeline computes true cost-adjusted non-Medicare bad debt (Cost of Uncompensated Care − Cost of Charity Care = $7.68B at non-profits vs. $21.48B charity care cost) so every ratio on this page is strictly apples-to-apples.

Limitations: Worksheet S-10 does not capture IRS Form 990 Schedule H community-building grants, research, or health-professions education—nor does it capture state-specific charity mandates (e.g., WA, CA, NY, IL, NJ, CO).
