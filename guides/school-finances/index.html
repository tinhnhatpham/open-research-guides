2. The Post-COVID Public School Enrollment Cliff (Steepest Large-District Drops, FY2019–FY2024)

Across matched U.S. districts, public K–12 enrollment fell by 1.65 million students (-3.4%) between FY2019 and FY2024. Among large districts (≥25,000 students in 2019), the steepest declines ran -16% to -22%—driving sharp spikes in fixed-cost per-pupil spending (+35% to +73%) as districts now face painful school consolidation budgets after federal ESSER relief expired.

4. Honest Caveats & Open Dataset Downloads

Important Methodological Notes:
  • ESSER Pandemic Funds in FY2024: FY2024 was the final major spending year of federal COVID-19 ESSER relief, temporarily elevating per-pupil operating spending and federal revenue shares in many districts relative to FY2019.
  • Regional Cost of Living & Special Education: Higher per-pupil spending in NY, NJ, MA, CT, and CA partly reflects higher regional teacher salaries, healthcare/pension costs, and dense special-education services—not simply "waste." Likewise, tiny rural districts naturally show higher administrative % due to fixed superintendent/principal overhead spread across few students (which is why our High Admin preset filters to ≥1,000 students).
  • Non-Operating Agencies Excluded: We filter out zero-enrollment intermediate service agencies (e.g., BOCES/ESAs) so every district row reflects an operating K–12 school system.
