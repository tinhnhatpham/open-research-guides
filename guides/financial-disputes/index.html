2. CFPB Complaint Outcomes by Product (2024–2025 Reports)

Notice the dramatic gap between complaint volume and actual monetary relief: credit bureaus account for ~85% of all CFPB complaints, yet almost never pay cash (their relief is fixing records). Meanwhile, credit card disputes win monetary relief ~13% of the time.

3. Data Literacy Warning: "Credit Washing" Spam & 2026 Database Changes

Why raw CFPB credit-bureau counts exploded: Independent fraud analysts found that a massive share of recent CFPB complaints against Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion come from copy-pasted legalistic templates ("credit washing" scripts) submitted by shady credit-repair services promising to erase valid negative debt. Never hand your logins to a paid "credit sweep" firm—you have a free federal right under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) to dispute errors directly, and spam templates often trigger automated rejection filters.
Mid-2026 CFPB Data Change: Analysts working with the CFPB database should note that the agency ceased publishing new public consumer narrative text in mid-2026; structured metadata (product, issue, company response, timeliness) remains the reliable dataset for tracking trends.

4. Primary Sources

  1. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB): Consumer Response Annual Reports (2024–2025) & Consumer Complaint Database structured outcomes (consumerfinance.gov/complaint).
  2. Federal Regulation E (Electronic Fund Transfer Act) & Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA): Legal distinction between unauthorized electronic fund transfers vs. socially engineered authorized transfers, and free direct credit-bureau dispute rights.
  3. Industry & Consumer Protection Analyses: Legal compliance summaries of CFPB resolution rates by product (Orrick, Hudson Cook, Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor) and fraud-analytics reviews of copy-paste "credit washing" spam.
